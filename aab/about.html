About

Economics first, risk second, plain language always.

I'm Rony — an economics graduate from Chittagong who went to Brighton to study finance and risk, and came back convinced that most financial knowledge fails people at the language step, not the math step.

The story so far

I studied Economics at the University of Chittagong, then moved to the UK for an MSc in Finance and Risk Management at the University of Brighton's School of Business and Law, finishing with an Upper Merit. Along the way the interests sharpened: corporate finance, equity research, credit risk and the question of how markets actually behave when you test them with data rather than describe them with adjectives.

My research followed that instinct. For my dissertation I spent 15,000 words examining whether Islamic funds genuinely exhibit lower risk than conventional UK funds — looking at systematic risk, idiosyncratic risk and drawdowns rather than taking the marketing at face value. In a related project I constructed an Islamic fund portfolio in Python and compared its pre- and post-COVID performance against conventional benchmarks, automating the analysis and the charts. I also worked through a credit-risk case study the way a bank would: liquidity, leverage, profitability, repayment capacity, recommendation.

Outside the numbers: I interned as a quantitative analysis intern with the Summit Equity Society (University of Essex), got a close look at financial fraud investigation during industry experience at a corporate legal firm in Eastbourne, and served as President of the Bangladeshi Society at Brighton. I also worked UK service jobs through my degree — the kind of work that teaches you calm under pressure better than any module does. Before all of this: Sylhet Cadet College, and a successful pass through the Bangladesh Navy officers' ISSB selection — discipline has a long backstory here.

This site exists because financial education in Bangladesh mostly arrives in English or in textbook Bangla, and neither is how people actually talk about money. The Learn hub is my attempt to fix that: every concept in everyday spoken Bangla, every term linked, no jargon left unexplained.

How I work

Assumptions live in one place

Every model I build has a single assumptions tab, colour-coded, with nothing hard-coded into a formula. You should be able to change a growth rate and watch the whole thing move — without calling me.

A number you can't trace is a number you can't defend

If a figure appears in a summary, it links back to the calculation and the calculation links back to the source. That's the difference between analysis and decoration.

Plain language is a technical skill

Anyone can restate jargon in more jargon. Explaining a concept so an intelligent non-specialist gets it — without making it wrong — takes understanding the thing properly first.

Say the uncertainty out loud

Risk management taught me the useful habit: state the range, name what would have to be true, and flag which input the answer is most sensitive to. Certainty that isn't there helps nobody.

Questions people actually ask
Why write investment education in Bangla?

Because the decisions get made in Bangla. A saver deciding between sanchayapatra and an FDR is not going to read a 40-page English circular, and shouldn't have to. Almost everything published on Bangladeshi markets is either English-language and technical, or Bangla and promotional. There's very little in between — that gap is what this site is for.

Do you give investment advice?

No, and not as a legal formality — as a design decision. I'm not a licensed adviser, I don't know your situation, and the moment a site like this starts recommending instruments it acquires an interest in you believing it. Explaining how something works and telling you what to buy are different jobs; I only do the first.

What does the dissertation actually say?

It compared Islamic and conventional fund performance, looking at systematic risk and drawdowns across the pre- and post-COVID periods. A readable summary is on the way to the Insights page — the short version is that the interesting differences show up in how the two behave under stress, not in headline average returns.

Are you available for work?

Yes — financial modeling, data analysis and finance writing, on a freelance basis. The portfolio page sets out what that covers and how a project runs, and the contact form reaches me directly.

How was this site built?

By hand, with no framework and no build step — and the whole thing is written up on the colophon page, including why the choices were made and what they cost.
