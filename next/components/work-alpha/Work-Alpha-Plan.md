# Work-Alpha

Month 1: from zero to an application-ready research pack

**End goal.** By Sunday 27 September 2026 you hold a sendable PhD application pack: a chosen research question, 15 verified papers with structured notes, a gap matrix, one dataset explored with a figure and a table, a 15-name supervisor directory with deadlines, a 2-page proposal that survived a hostile review, and three outreach emails ready to send.

Sat and Sun, 10 hours a week, from 2026-09-05.

## The seven checkpoints

| Goal | Name | End of day | The test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| G1 | Decision made, tools ready | 1 | One track chosen with a written 3-line reason. Zotero, Drive tree, Colab and the prompt library all open in one click. |
| G2 | Literature map | 2 | 15 papers verified (DOI or PDF in hand), 8 venues recorded, reading order fixed. |
| G3 | Ten papers read, gap found | 4 | 10 structured notes complete, gap matrix filled, one sentence naming the gap. |
| G4 | Data touched | 5 | One dataset loaded in Colab, four sanity checks passed, one figure and one summary table saved. |
| G5 | People and dates | 6 | 15 supervisors ranked A/B/C, scholarship rounds dated, five loose ends assigned. |
| G6 | Proposal survives review | 7 | 2-page proposal v2 exists and the hostile reviewer's top three objections are answered in the text. |
| G7 | Pack ready to send | 8 | Proposal final, CV bullets updated, three emails drafted, month 2 plan written. |

## Two tracks (decide on Day 1, task 3)

### Track A: Agricultural and climate risk economics

- Question: Do rainfall and temperature shocks reduce farm household consumption and income in Bangladesh, and does access to credit or index insurance buffer the effect?

- Venues: Journal of Development Economics; American Journal of Agricultural Economics; World Development; Food Policy; Economic Development and Cultural Change; Environment and Development Economics; AEJ: Applied Economics; Journal of Development Studies
- Where to find supervisors: Search OpenAlex and Google Scholar for authors with 3+ papers since 2020 on 'index insurance', 'weather shocks', 'Bangladesh agriculture'. - Open Work-Alpha, read today's goal card out loud

### Day 1: 2026-09-05, Decide and set up (G1 Decision made, tools ready, 300 min)

**Why.** Every later task assumes these five things exist. Doing them once, together, removes every 'where do I put this' moment for the rest of the month.

**Do exactly this.**

**Hand in.** Drive tree exists, Zotero installed, Colab notebook saved, one Scholar alert, one bookmark folder.

**Done means.** You can open all five in under a minute from the bookmark folder.

## Prompt library

### Paper Note

When: Every paper you read closely (Days 3 and 4)

```
You are my reading assistant. Here are the abstract, introduction and conclusion of a paper. Answer under exactly these headings and nothing else: Question; Data (source, years, N); Method (one sentence); Main result (with the number); Identification worry; What it leaves open; One sentence I could cite it for. Under 40 words per heading. Write 'not stated' rather than guessing. Do not add headings.

[PASTE TEXT]
```

### Hostile reviewer

When: Any gap sentence or proposal draft. Always in a fresh chat with nothing else pasted.

```
You are a sceptical referee for a top field journal in economics. You have not seen anything about this project before. Read the text below and give: the three objections most likely to lead to rejection, ranked; for each, the specific sentence or paragraph it targets and what evidence would remove it; one thing the author claims that the text does not support; and one strength, in one line. Be blunt, be specific, do not soften.

[PASTE TEXT]
```

### Section drafter

When: Drafting one proposal section from its outline sentence (Day 7)

```
Draft the [SECTION NAME] section of a two-page PhD research proposal in economics. It must carry this sentence: [SENTENCE]. Use only these sources, cited as (Author Year): [LIST]. 100 to 160 words, formal, no adverbs, no rhetorical questions, no 'in today's world'. If a claim needs a citation I have not given, write [CITATION NEEDED] rather than inventing one.
```

### Outreach email

When: Positioning paragraphs and emails (Days 6 and 8)

```
Write an email to Professor [SURNAME] from a prospective PhD student. Rules: under 200 words; the first sentence must refer to a specific finding in their [YEAR] paper [TITLE]; the second states my question in one sentence: [QUESTION]; the third says what data I bring: [DATA]; exactly one ask, a 20-minute call in the next month; no adverbs, no flattery, no 'I am passionate'. Sign as [NAME], MSc [DEGREE], [UNIVERSITY]. Their recent papers: [PASTE].
```

### Colab code

When: Any data task (Day 5)

```
Write Python for Google Colab using pandas and matplotlib. Task: [DESCRIBE]. Data: [FILE, COLUMNS]. Rules: one cell per step with a one-line comment saying what a wrong output would look like; never modify the raw dataframe, always assign to a new name; print shapes after every merge or filter; save every figure at 300 dpi to [PATH] with the file name [YYYY-MM-DD_short-description_v1.png].
```

### Paper substitute

When: A seed paper cannot be found (Day 2)

```
I could not find this paper: [CITATION]. Suggest three substitutes published 2015 or later on the same question in a well-regarded field journal, giving authors, year, journal and a one-line reason each. Do not invent papers: if you are not sure a paper exists, say so and give a search string I can use on Google Scholar instead.
```
